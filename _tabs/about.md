I have always been drawn to physics and astronomy, the kind of curiosity that makes you want to understand not just what things are, but how they work at a fundamental level.

Spinflip started from a simple question: could I actually detect a signal from interstellar hydrogen with hardware that costs less than a dinner out? The answer, it turns out, is yes, and the process of getting there taught me more about radio engineering, signal processing, and the physics of the galaxy than I had expected.

This site documents that project. If you are curious about radio astronomy or want to build something similar, the code is open source at [github.com/nekhebit/spinflip](https://github.com/nekhebit/spinflip).

None of this would have happened without [CHART](https://github.com/astrochart/CHART), the Completely Hackable Amateur Radio Telescope project. Finding that project was what made me realise that detecting the hydrogen line at home was actually possible, and spinflip grew directly out of following their work.
